# Windows, doors, and opening joinery

Start with the current opening schedule and evaluated wall geometry. Record rough width/head/sill, wall depth, finished-floor datum, frame allowances, and the requested fixed, hinged, sliding, or open-passage type. A specified leaf height is not automatically the rough-opening height. Preserve approved hinge side, leaf count, and pose; an open passage does not imply a door or hardware.

Use the floor-plan skill for new wall-shell/opening-layout construction. This reference fits assemblies to those openings; a trim correction is not permission to relocate an opening or rebuild the wall.

## Fit components while preserving their sections

Model frame, sash or leaf, glass, lining, sill, and hardware according to their functions. Keep moving parts separate with suitable pivots. Use economical polygon construction for straight joinery and editable paths/sections for profiles. An architectural assembly does not require a single welded subdivision mesh or a uniform TurboSmooth stack. Apply subdivision only where a curved part benefits from it.

When resizing, adjust paths, member lengths, and panel fields while preserving specified member widths, relief, and bevels. Avoid scaling the entire assembly to fix a return depth. Read each opening rather than assuming all copies have identical dimensions. For operable parts, inspect clearance about the actual hinge or sliding path, including nearby hardware; preserve the requested final pose after any authorized movement test.

## Resolve the complete section

Check the receiving wall, finished reveal face, casing's inner contour, frame rebate, and sill together. Fit visible and concealed backing within the available depth. A correctly placed spline or hidden strip does not prove the finished face meets its neighbor. Keep functional joints and designed setbacks, but do not invent gaps to conceal a fitting error.

Fit glazing to its actual rebate and intended thickness. Coordinate sill bearing, front projection, and ends with the reference; projecting horns are a design option, not universally required or forbidden. Preserve real entry slots for pocket/sliding systems instead of closing their travel path with a continuous lining.

Where the design uses plinth blocks under casings, size each block for the full supported trim/jamb footprint. Terminate the casing at the block top and the baseboard at its side, avoiding buried duplicate feet and overlapping volumes. A simple closed element is often sufficient; attachment does not require welding physically separate construction parts.

At a tight perpendicular return, measure the whole section and support footprint. Preserve the opening unless its adjustment is authorized. Choose a compatible section or a clean scribed termination within the brief. If a wall or opening change is required, explain the conflict before dependent work. An authorized relocation must coordinate jambs, head, assembly, threshold, baseboard ends, and editable sources, with circulation and nearby furniture rechecked.

## Preserve and verify

Discover shared geometry, profiles, materials, and maps before editing. Make the intended copy independent when a local correction would otherwise affect unrelated openings. Retain useful sources and material IDs; attach only the assemblies the task requires. Do not collapse an approved stack merely to inspect its evaluated shape.

Inspect the complete head and verticals from both room sides, then close views of sill/reveal and casing/plinth/baseboard junctions in context. Isolation helps expose detail but cannot prove clearance to walls. Check normals, visible profile continuity, material roles, and mapping after fitting through [materials and review](materials-and-review.md). Preserve the scene's renderer and finishes; a neutral modeling material or wood/glass/metal split is a project choice, not a fixed requirement.
