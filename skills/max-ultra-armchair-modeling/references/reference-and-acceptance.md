# Reference Evidence and Visual Acceptance

## Evidence before vertices

Open and visually inspect the supplied photographs and drawings, not only product-page text. Record the product/version and 5-10 identifying features: for example back rake and thickness, seat contour, armrest plan contour and sections, leg stance, gaps, and rail positions. Use a compact record with feature, dimension/observation, source view, confidence, and status. Keep manufacturer measurements separate from estimates derived from perspective photographs. Do not measure perspective pixels as though they came from an orthographic drawing.

When a concrete feature remains uncertain, proactively seek additional same-product/same-version photographs, customer images, top/side/back closeups, assembly instructions, videos, and available interactive 3D or 360 viewers. State the question the next source should answer, inspect its relevant view, and return to construction once the evidence is adequate. Searching must neither become endless nor excuse overlooking adequate originals. Viewer geometry can clarify form even when its topology is poor; cross-check fidelity against photographs and dimensions, and never copy its topology blindly.

Hidden fastening details may remain disclosed estimates. An unresolved visible identifying contour needs better evidence or a concrete user clarification before accepting that component. Conflicting dimensions need resolution, not silent averaging.

## Coupled dimensions

Choose axes, floor plane, center, and front direction. Establish overall extrema, floor contacts, seat edges, and back/armrest attachment landmarks. Couple:

- Overall width to seat width, side gaps, armrest widths, and frame spacing.
- Seat height to filling thickness, base, and supporting rail position.
- Seat depth to back rake, back thickness, and seat/back contact.
- Front and rear leg stance to floor contacts and armrest supports.

Adjust the responsible dimensions and sections together. Do not use independent arbitrary constants or stretch the assembled chair along X/Y/Z merely to match a bounding box; that changes radii, thicknesses, slopes, and fit.

## Visual gates

Before seams, fasteners, UVs, or materials, compare the primary silhouette AND each distinctive component. Match viewing side, camera height, perspective, and framing to a reference photograph. Free viewport matching is sufficient unless a camera is needed; use the camera-composition skill when creating or modifying a camera. Do not distort the model to compensate for a mismatched camera. Comparison images may be cropped, translated, and uniformly scaled, never independently stretched along their axes.

Show reference and model side by side; a contour overlay can supplement comparison. For every identifying feature record `matches available evidence`, `needs correction`, or `unconfirmed`, with the inspected source/capture and specific deviation. No feature passes merely because its total length or overall bounding box agrees. Inspect armrest contour, thickness, and highlight flow separately. A rounded rectangular plank fails when the reference has an elongated rounded form with varying width and section, even if its topology is mechanically valid.

Known substantial contour or section errors block progression to details. Revise the construction approach when repeated minor tweaks do not improve likeness; adding seams or polygons to the wrong form is not progress. Continue autonomously through these internal gates without mandatory user approval at each stage.

Review unsmoothed cage and one-iteration smooth views with matching framing: front and side orthographic for proportions, three-quarter for volume, rear and underside for construction, and closeups of armrest endings/supports, cushion edges, joints, and requested seams. Check clearance and support after smoothing as well as before it. Hidden joinery penetration can be intentional; visible accidental interpenetration is a defect.

Keep structural and visual findings separate in the handoff. Report unresolved visible differences, uncertain hidden details, and which views were actually checked. A successful operation or mesh validator proves neither an exact copy nor stock readiness. Substantial known likeness errors mean the reconstruction remains incomplete.
